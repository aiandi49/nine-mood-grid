Pipeline

Three steps, no design work

Everything between "upload" and "download" happens automatically — the prompt-writing is invisible to the person using it.

01 / Read

Upload a photo

A vision model scans the image for its own visual language — fabric, jewelry, hair, palette, light — the raw material for the surrounding tiles.

02 / Write

Prompt is generated

Eight tile descriptions are drafted and merged into a single combined prompt, tuned to one consistent color palette and lighting direction.

03 / Render

Grid comes back

The prompt and reference photo are sent to an image-generation model. The finished 3×3 grid returns — the prompt stays behind the scenes.

Permissions

Real photo or avatar — consent is the actual line

Generators don't reliably tell synthetic from real. What actually matters — for your users and for platform policy — is whether the person in the photo has agreed to it being used this way.

Build this in

  • A required confirmation at upload: "This is me, or I have permission to use this photo."
  • Clear terms stating users are responsible for having rights to any photo they upload.
  • Separate consent language for photos containing more than one identifiable person.
  • A way to report or remove a grid made from someone's likeness without their consent.

Don't rely on

  • Detecting "AI-generated vs. real" as a stand-in for consent — most tools can't do this reliably, and it isn't the actual issue.
  • One platform's permissiveness as a signal that a photo is safe to use — policies vary and change without notice.
  • Silence as consent. No confirmation, no upload.
  • Treating a stated "it's an avatar" claim as unverifiable-but-irrelevant — it still shapes how you should label and moderate the result.
NOTE — This is general product-safety guidance, not legal advice. Publicity-rights and privacy law vary by country and U.S. state. Talk to a lawyer about consent and likeness requirements before launching this for other people's photos.
